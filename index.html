Behold, the cosmos

The three musics. Boethius divided all music in three: musica mundana, the inaudible harmony of the cosmos itself; musica humana, the harmony of body and soul; and musica instrumentalis, the music instruments make. This device is a bridge — it takes the first and renders it as the third, for the benefit of the second. You.

The ladder of the spheres. Pythagoras found that the consonant intervals are simple ratios of string length — halve a string, hear the octave. The ancients stacked those same ratios up the heavens, from the Earth beneath your feet to Saturn, the farthest light the old sky knew. This engine uses the classical ascent, and it spans exactly one octave — the diapason:

Retrograde. Sometimes a planet appears to stop against the stars and run backward for weeks. Nothing truly reverses — it is Earth overtaking a slower world, the way a passed car seems to slide backward. The old astrologers read it as a turning-inward: review, delay, interiority. Here, a retrograde planet withdraws to the inner dashed ring, and its voice trembles.

Why the place matters. The zodiac positions are the same seen from anywhere on Earth. But the horizon is yours alone. Your latitude and longitude decide which bodies stand overhead and which lie beneath the earth — and in this instrument, presence follows altitude. Two people at the same moment on opposite sides of the world hear the same chord, voiced entirely differently.

The Moon's secret. The phase of the Moon is not a property of the Moon at all — it is the angle between the Moon and the Sun. New moon is their conjunction; full moon their opposition; the quarters their squares. It is the only aspect in the whole sky visible to the naked eye, and everyone who has ever lived has watched it without knowing its name. Here the Moon rules the air itself: at the new moon the sound is close, dark and thin; as she waxes the veil lifts, the body returns, and the room opens vast and ringing.

How true is this? Positions come from a modern ephemeris accurate to about one arc-minute — a sixtieth of a degree — over thousands of years. The Ascendant is computed from the sidereal rotation of the Earth for your exact coordinates. The clock conversion honours the full historical time-zone record, including daylight saving as it actually was. The sky you hear is the sky that stood there.
